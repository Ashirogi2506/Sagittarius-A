# Earth mission glossary

- **Budget:** The amount of game currency available to purchase instruments, spacecraft systems, and launch services.
- **Mass:** How much matter the spacecraft contains, measured in kilograms. A launcher can only carry a limited mass to the selected orbit.
- **Power supply:** Electrical power produced by systems such as solar panels, measured in watts.
- **Power demand:** Electrical power consumed by instruments and spacecraft systems, measured in watts.
- **Data generation rate:** How quickly instruments create data, measured in megabits per second.
- **Data storage:** How much data the spacecraft can retain before transmitting it, measured in gigabits.
- **Communications capacity:** The maximum rate at which the spacecraft can send data, measured in megabits per second.
- **Mission duration:** The planned operating time of the mission, measured in days.
- **Scientific value:** A gameplay score estimating how well the design can achieve the mission objective.
- **Reliability:** The estimated chance that a system continues operating as intended.
- **Risk:** The estimated chance or severity of mission problems. It is simplified for gameplay and shown as a percentage.
- **Orbit altitude:** The spacecraft's height above Earth's surface, measured in kilometres.
- **Image resolution:** The smallest surface detail an instrument can distinguish. Smaller ground-sample distance means finer detail.
- **Surface coverage:** The amount of Earth's surface the mission can observe over a period of time.
- **Compatibility rule:** A requirement connecting two choices, such as launcher capacity being greater than spacecraft mass.
- **Validation error:** A problem that prevents launch until the design changes.
- **Validation warning:** A risky or inefficient choice that still allows launch.
- **Downlink:** Transmission of stored spacecraft data to a ground station.
- **Payload:** The instruments or cargo carried by the spacecraft or launcher.

These definitions are player-facing summaries. Exact formulas and scientifically sourced explanations must be supplied by the simulation and science members before final balancing.
