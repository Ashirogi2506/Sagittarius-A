# Moon playable slice

The `MoonEnvironment.fbx` environment is an experimental export of `FarSignal-Moon-Pole7.blend`. The untouched source is in `SourceArt/Moon/`. It preserves evaluated collection instances, including Beacon pad, cart and Pole-7. `Tools/Blender/export_moon_environment.py` documents the export. Blender 4.5 reports that the source was written by a newer version, so artwork and materials require visual comparison with the authoring Blender version before being approved.

In the Unity editor, open `Assets/Scenes/GameplayUI.unity`, enter Play mode and choose **MOON SURFACE TEST** from the main menu. This editor-only shortcut uses an isolated in-memory handoff and does not modify the campaign save. In the intended campaign flow, the arrival screen's **LAND ON MOON** uses the actual Earth handoff. The cargo, power and landing minigame runners are still required to reach it normally.

Controls: WASD movement, Space to jump, mouse look, left click to use/interact, right click to pick up, Escape unlocks the cursor, click to relock, H reopens the task explanation, Tab returns to the UI. E is an additional use/interact key. Left click to confirm the saved landing plan, then follow the objective and orange interaction markers. The cart sequence is start, power off, open cover, clean three patches, close cover, retest, then pick up and load the panel and kit one at a time. The Moon movement uses 1.62 m/s² gravity; later locations need their own movement settings, and an orbiter in microgravity will need dedicated movement rather than an ordinary ground jump.

Only four major milestones show an automatic explanation: arrival, wheel repair, the service route and Pole-7 repair. Small actions start immediately; H opens their explanation when needed. Begin Task proceeds; Back/Escape preserves progress. Leaving a battery has a separate confirmation. Each repair then opens a 2D panel: cover bolts use a cross sequence, dust is brushed off with a mouse drag, diagnostics use a green-range timing test, circuit isolation uses ordered switches and essential power uses the supplied cable-routing grid (essential services/local radio required; research/lights optional), cables connect matching letters/colours, the optional laser traces a weld seam, the P-4 panel snaps into its socket and needs four latches, and the relay uses three tuning sliders. Route upload has a short ordered verification panel. Escape or X cancels the current attempt without completing it; completed tasks are saved. World movement and interactions stop while a panel is open. Mistakes provide feedback and allow a retry without consuming mission resources.

`MoonPlayableExperience` builds the surface at runtime. After loading the cart, use its switch to travel to the bridge, inspect it, survey the firm route, return to the cart switch and travel to Pole-7. Deliver the supplies, isolate and repair the circuit, install the panel, restore essential power and the relay, upload the route, then use the cart to return to the lander. Optional markers let the player inspect the steep route and dead beacon; a carried battery can be left there. The HUD gives distance and direction to the main objective. The cart follows an automatic waypoint route, not player steering; the player can look around with the mouse while riding.

Progress is saved in the Moon draft, with older cart-only saves migrated. The results screen leads to a Mars staging summary; `GetMarsHandoff()` preserves the commander, build, remaining fuel and outcomes, excluding the delivered panel and any battery left behind. Mars gameplay is not built yet. The isolated test uses an in-memory save; exiting it discards that test run.

`MoonCartRoutePlanner` searches clear ground between surveyed checkpoints, checking a 4.2-metre-wide envelope against rocks, rails, structures and slopes. `MoonCartMotor` rounds corners only when that clearance remains valid, turns toward travel, accelerates and brakes, rotates/steers the wheels and samples four ground contacts for pitch and roll. The rider can look around independently and dismounts on clear ground beside the cart. Travel is committed only at arrival; exiting mid-trip resumes from the last completed stop. This remains automatic transport, not manual driving or a physics vehicle simulation.

Editor checks (Play mode, main menu): **Far Signal > Run Moon Repair Panel Checks** exercises all 13 task variants with mouse input, cancellation and wrong-input cases. **Run Moon Mouse Interaction Flow** uses real raycast/mouse input through the complete mission, positioning the player near each target to keep it quick. **Run Moon Surface Flow Checks** and **Run Moon Ridge Flow Checks** validate travel and mission state on both pads; they submit repair results directly and do not substitute for the panel checks.

The route uses authored corridors and simplified interaction markers; final 3D repair animations, sound, full campaign-wide saving, failed-descent checkpoint restoration and the teammate-owned Earth minigames remain integration work. Both Beacon and Ridge have spawn positioning and automated route checks; a complete manual walking playthrough is still useful. The source was created by a newer Blender version, so the export also needs art review.
